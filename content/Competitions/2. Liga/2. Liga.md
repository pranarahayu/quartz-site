The 2. Liga is the second-highest division of the Sæløsk football league system. While every club in Ligaen operates on a full-time professional basis, 2. Liga contains a mixture of professional and semi-professional clubs.

The division is contested by ten clubs and is administered directly by the Sæløernes Boldspil-Sammenslutning (SBS). The champion is promoted automatically to Ligaen, while the runner-up enters a play-off against the ninth-placed Ligaen club. At the other end of the table, the bottom club is relegated automatically to 3. Liga, and the ninth-placed club enters a play-off against the 3. Liga runner-up.

### History

#### Establishment and re-election

The competition was established in 1952 as the **1. Division**, becoming the first nationwide second tier of the Sæløsk football league system. Its introduction created a formal division beneath the eight-team Mesterrækken and marked the first expansion of the national league pyramid beyond the top flight.

The inaugural 1. Division contained eight clubs. Promotion to the Mesterrækken was not determined automatically by league position. Instead, movement between the two divisions was controlled through the controversial **Genvalgssystemet**.

Under the system, the seven highest-placed Mesterrækken clubs voted on the composition of the top division for the following season. This allowed established top-flight members to decide whether the lowest-placed Mesterrækken club should retain its place or be replaced by a leading club from the 1. Division.

The arrangement strongly favoured existing Mesterrækken clubs and restricted movement between the divisions. Only two clubs gained promotion through re-election between the introduction of the 1. Division in 1952 and the abolition of the system in 1965.

#### Automatic promotion and relegation

The Genvalgssystemet was abolished ahead of the 1966 season as part of a wider restructuring of Sæløsk football. The Mesterrækken was renamed the Sæløernes Hovedliga and expanded from eight to ten clubs.

Two automatic promotion and relegation places were introduced between the Hovedliga and the 1. Division. League position therefore became the sole sporting basis for movement between the two highest levels.

A third national tier, named the 2. Division, was created at the same time. It initially contained eight clubs divided between two regional groups, which competed for one promotion place to the 1. Division. The pyramid was expanded again in 1982. The 1. Division and the 2. Division increased from to twelve clubs each.

#### Renaming and reorganization

A major reorganization took place in 1993. The top flight was formally professionalized and renamed Ligaen. The 1. Division adopted its present name, **2. Liga**, while the former third-tier 2. Division became 3. Liga.

The restructuring standardized the top three levels at ten clubs each. Unlike Ligaen, which was transferred to the newly established Ligaforeningen, 2. Liga remained under the direct administration of the SBS.

The growth of professional football nevertheless affected the character of the second tier. Several clubs adopted full-time playing structures, while others continued to operate on a semi-professional basis. This mixture remains a defining feature of the division.

In 2004, 2. Liga adopted a quadruple round-robin schedule. Each club played every opponent four times, twice at home and twice away, producing a 36-match season.

#### Introduction of the play-offs

Promotion and relegation were revised in 2020. The two automatic promotion places between 2. Liga and Ligaen were replaced by one automatic place and one play-off place.

Under the revised system, the 2. Liga champion is promoted automatically. The runner-up faces the ninth-placed Ligaen club in a promotion and relegation play-off, with the winner securing a place in the following season's Ligaen.

The same arrangement was introduced at the lower end of 2. Liga. The bottom-placed club is relegated automatically to 3. Liga, while the ninth-placed club faces the 3. Liga runner-up. The 3. Liga champion is promoted automatically.

### Competition format

The division consists of ten clubs. Each club plays every other team four times during the season, twice at home and twice away, for a total of 36 matches.

The club finishing first is declared 2. Liga champion and is promoted automatically to Ligaen. The second-placed club enters the promotion play-off against ninth place in Ligaen.

The tenth-placed club is relegated automatically to 3. Liga. The ninth-placed club enters a play-off against the 3. Liga runner-up to determine which club will compete in 2. Liga during the following season.

### Administration

The competition is administered by the SBS rather than the Ligaforeningen. The SBS is responsible for its fixture schedule, competition regulations, refereeing, disciplinary proceedings and club licensing.

Although some 2. Liga clubs operate professionally, they do not become members of the Ligaforeningen unless promoted to Ligaen.
