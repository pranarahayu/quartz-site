Association football is the most popular sport in Sæløerne, an autonomous territory within the Kingdom of Denmark. Approximately 60% of people who participate in organized sport in the territory play football.

Football is governed nationally by the [[Sæløernes Boldspil-Sammenslutning (SBS)]]. Despite Sæløerne not being a sovereign state, the association has held membership of FIFA since 1985 and UEFA since 1987.

The domestic game is organized through a four-level national league system, the _[Sælmesterskabet](Sælmesterskabet)_ national cup and six regional football associations. The [[Ligaforeningen]] administers the professional [Ligaen](Ligaen), while the lower national divisions remain under the direct administration of the SBS.

### History

#### Introduction and early clubs

Football was introduced to Sæløerne during the late 19th century by Danish merchant sailors and naval personnel. Early matches were informal and were generally played on beaches or rough coastal pastures, as few settlements possessed suitable sporting grounds.

The first organized club was Dansk Boldklub, founded in Kyststed in 1904 by Danish sailors and local residents. Its formation prompted members of the local university's gymnastics club, together with scholars and teachers, to establish Kyststed Universitets Gymnastik- og Boldspilforening (KUGF). Other clubs were subsequently formed in communities elsewhere in the territory.

The early game remained concentrated around Kyststed. In 1907, the Hovedstadens Sports Association began organizing regular football tournaments in the capital region. Responsibility for these competitions passed to the newly established Hovedstadens Fodboldforbund (HFF) in 1910, which organized the annual Kyststed Hovedstad Championship.

#### Regional era

Football outside the capital developed under five additional regional bodies: Nordmarkens Fodboldforbund (NFF), Østlandets Fodboldforbund (ØFF), Sønderkystens Fodboldforbund (SøFF), Vestskærgårdens Fodboldforbund (VFF) and Luffes Fodboldforbund (LFF). Each association organized its own championship, and clubs competed primarily within their respective regions.

The first island-wide competition was the _[Sælmesterskabet](Sælmesterskabet)_, first contested in 1918. The knockout tournament brought together the champions of all six regions, as well as the runners-up and third-placed club from the Kyststed Hovedstad Championship.

The quarter-final draw paired each of the three Kyststed representatives with a regional champion, while the remaining two regional champions faced one another. Capital clubs were generally stronger than their provincial opponents, and three of the four semi-finalists frequently came from Kyststed Hovedstad.

Although the tournament produced an overall Sæløsk champion, it did not constitute a unified league. The regional associations remained independent, with no central authority responsible for common competition rules or movement between regions.

#### Formation of the SBS

Growing interest in football during the 1930s increased support for a national governing body. The Sæløernes Boldspil-Sammenslutning was founded in 1939 to standardize regulations and coordinate football across the territory.

The SBS introduced the [Mesterrækken](Ligaen) in 1942 as Sæløerne's first national league. Its inaugural season contained eight clubs: three from Kyststed Hovedstad and one from each of the other five regions. The teams played a double round-robin schedule of fourteen matches.

The establishment of the Mesterrækken ended the use of the _Sælmesterskabet_ to determine the principal national champion. Regional competitions nevertheless continued alongside the national league.

In 1957, the SBS revived the _Sælmesterskabet_ as the national knockout cup. Earlier winners of the competition were thereafter recorded as cup winners rather than national league champions, including those from the period before the formation of the Mesterrækken.

### International football

The SBS was admitted to FIFA on 10 October 1985 and joined UEFA on 17 May 1987. Before receiving international recognition, the Sæløsk national team played occasional friendly matches against representative teams from the Faroe Islands, Åland, and Iceland.

Sæløerne entered the qualifying competitions for the UEFA European Championship and FIFA World Cup in 1988. Its first competitive match ended in a 5–0 defeat to Sweden in a 1990 FIFA World Cup qualifier on 31 August 1988.

The national team's first competitive victory came later in the same qualifying campaign, when Sæløerne defeated Albania 2–1. The result is widely regarded as one of the most significant matches in the history of the Sæløsk national team.

Sæløsk clubs became eligible to enter UEFA competitions following the SBS's admission in 1987. Clubs have since appeared in the European Cup and its successor, the UEFA Champions League; the UEFA Cup and its successor, the UEFA Europa League; the UEFA Cup Winners' Cup; and the UEFA Conference League.

### League system

The Sæløsk football league system consists of four national levels. The top three levels are unified divisions, while the fourth-tier Lokalligaen is divided into northern and southern groups.

The [Ligaforeningen](Ligaforeningen%20\(LF\)) administers Ligaen on behalf of its ten member clubs. The SBS directly administers 2. Liga, 3. Liga and the Lokalligaen. The six regional associations remain responsible for regional, grassroots and youth football.

Ligaen is fully professional. The second tier includes both professional and semi-professional clubs, while the third and fourth tiers consist mainly of semi-professional and amateur teams.

#### Development

A second national level was introduced in 1952 as the **1. Division**. Both the Mesterrækken and the new division initially contained eight clubs.

Movement between the two divisions was controlled through the **Genvalgssystemet**, a re-election system under which the top seven clubs in the Mesterrækken voted on which clubs would compete in the division during the following season. The system allowed established top-flight clubs to protect their positions and provided little opportunity for clubs from the second tier. Only two clubs gained promotion through re-election between 1952 and 1965.

The Genvalgssystemet was abolished before the 1966 season. The Mesterrækken was renamed the **Sæløernes Hovedliga** and expanded from eight to ten clubs, with one automatic promotion and relegation places introduced between the top two divisions.

A third national level, the **2. Division**, was created as part of the same reorganization. Its eight clubs were divided into two groups, which competed for one promotion place to the 1. Division.

The next major expansion took place in 1982. The 1. Division increased from eight to twelve clubs, extending its regular season to 22 matches per team. The 2. Division was also expanded to twelve clubs.

In 1993, the top flight became fully professional and was renamed [Ligaen](Ligaen). The 1. Division and 2. Division were renamed [2. Liga](2.%20Liga) and [3. Liga](3.%20Liga) respectively. The third tier's regional groups were replaced by a single national division, and the top three levels were standardized at ten clubs each.

The [Ligaforeningen](Ligaforeningen%20\(LF\)) was established during the same reorganization to represent the Ligaen clubs and administer the professional top division. The lower divisions remained under the SBS.

A championship and relegation split was initially used in Ligaen following the 1993 reorganization. It remained in place until 2004, when it was replaced by a quadruple round-robin schedule in which every club faced each opponent four times.

The [Lokalligaen](Lokalligaen) was introduced as the fourth national level in 2004. It was originally divided into four regional groups. In 2018, the competition was reorganized into two groups, Lokalligaen Nord and Lokalligaen Syd, each containing eight clubs.

A further change to promotion and relegation was introduced in 2020. Between Ligaen and 2. Liga, the bottom club in Ligaen was relegated automatically and replaced by the 2. Liga champion, while the ninth-placed Ligaen club entered a play-off against the 2. Liga runner-up.

The same system was introduced between 2. Liga and 3. Liga. The bottom club in 2. Liga was relegated automatically, while the ninth-placed club contested a play-off against the 3. Liga runner-up. The 3. Liga champion received automatic promotion. The reform replaced the previous system of two automatic promotion and relegation places at both levels.

### Youth football

Youth football is administered by the SBS in cooperation with the six regional associations. Competitions are organized across six age groups, with most early-stage football played regionally to limit travel between the islands.

The youth system serves both community clubs and the academies of clubs competing in the national divisions. Development standards introduced after the SBS gained international recognition have placed greater emphasis on coaching education, facilities and structured competition.
